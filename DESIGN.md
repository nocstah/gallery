# Design System: Noodle Gallery (local Omarchy layer)

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Tiled Workspace"**

The archive is a workspace you live in, drawn in the grammar of the owner's own Omarchy/Hyprland desktop. Years are workspaces you switch between; photos are windows tiled on one even gap; the theme accent is the active border and lights only what has focus. The chrome is the same ground as the content, split by 1px hairlines, so the photos stay the brightest thing on screen.

This layer is a citizen of the desktop, not a brand. **Colour is not owned here.** The palette (`--immich-ui-light`, `--immich-ui-dark`, `--immich-ui-muted`, `--immich-ui-gray`, `--immich-ui-default-border`, the `--immich-ui-primary-*` ramp, the `--color-gray-*` ramp, `--omarchy-accent`, `--omarchy-on-accent`) and the desktop geometry and font (`--ws-gap`, `--ws-radius`, `--ws-border`, `--omarchy-font-mono`) arrive at runtime as admin Custom CSS that `~/.local/bin/immich-omarchy-theme` generates from the active Omarchy theme and Hyprland config (run by the theme-set and font-set hooks). `web/src/styles/omarchy-world.css` owns type, geometry rules, material, components, layout and motion, and only reads those tokens. Every colour and geometry value in the frontmatter is the build's **fallback** (the light face of `gallery-theme.css`, and the `:root` defaults in `omarchy-world.css`), shown when the hook has not run. Never hard-code a value from one theme.

The layer began visual only: it styles upstream markup through stable hooks (`data-testid`, `data-thumbnail-focus-container`, ids) and `ws-` classes added to upstream components. It is now a deliberate fork (PRODUCT.md, Change scope) that also owns screens and flows: the Time Strip, the sidebar's Album Tree, its Pinned albums and photos carried to it, the Explorer of Tags and Folders, the Shared feed and the Share sheet, the special albums, Bank slips, the tool pages (Duplicates, Manage location, Large files, Workflows, Import), Album folders, the Space page, Photo books, the Adjust panel, the Memories cards and film, Room, and Curator's inbox and panels, each described under Components. A redesign may restructure a screen; every structural departure from upstream gets its entry under Components when it lands, so a merge of an upstream release knows what is ours.

**Key Characteristics:**
- One ground: the theme surface everywhere, divided by hairlines, never two-tone slabs.
- One gap module (`--ws-gap`, Hyprland `gaps_in` x2) spaces the tiles and derives the header rhythm.
- Tiles take the desktop's window radius and border width.
- Active state is inversion (ink fill, surface text), like the Omarchy bar's active workspace; the accent is reserved for focus.
- Scale contrast carries hierarchy: condensed heavy numerals at display size against 12px monospace data.
- Depth only on what floats (menus, dialogs, the floating grouping control).

## Colors

The palette is the active Omarchy theme, read at runtime; this system defines roles, not hues.

### Primary
- **Theme Accent** (`--ws-accent` = `var(--omarchy-accent, var(--immich-ui-primary-500))`; fallback in frontmatter `accent`): the active border. Used only for focus and response: the focused tile's border, `:focus-visible` outlines, text selection (26% mix), native `accent-color`, the hover underline of text links, and a 15% tint into the empty-state button on hover. Runtime source: the theme's accent from `colors.toml`, contrast-pushed by the hook.

### Neutral
- **Theme Surface** (`--immich-ui-light`; fallback `surface`): the single ground for page, top bar, sidebar, filter rail and floating controls. Also the text colour on inverted (active) elements.
- **Theme Ink** (`--immich-ui-dark`; fallback `ink`): primary text, display numerals, and the fill of every active/selected element.
- **Muted Ink** (`--immich-ui-muted`; fallback `muted`): secondary text: counts, inactive year pills, inactive segmented options, empty-state body. The hook pushes it to at least 4.6:1 against the surface.
- **Hairline** (`--ws-hairline` = ink at 13% in OKLab; fallback `hairline`): every divider, the tile's inactive border, control outlines, menu and dialog borders.
- **Caption Scrim / Caption Ink** (`caption-scrim`, `caption-ink`): fixed colours, and only on the photo itself: the bottom gradient (black 55% to transparent) and white text of the place caption. They sit on imagery, not on the theme. The scrim a phone's filter sheet lays over the photos beside it (black at 50%, as the map's drawer has) is the same kind of thing: a dim on imagery.
- **QR Black / QR White**: the other fixed pair, a shared link's QR code (`components/omarchy/ShareLinkPanel.svelte`), black on a white quiet zone whatever the theme: a phone's camera needs the contrast and the quiet zone, and a dark theme would run into the code's edge. Like the caption, it is something to read off the screen, not chrome.

### Named Rules
**The Desktop Decides Rule.** No colour literal enters the build except the on-photo scrims (the caption's, a sheet's over the photos) and a QR code's black on white. New surfaces take roles (`--immich-ui-*`, `--ws-accent`, `--ws-hairline`), never hex values from the theme you happen to be looking at.

**The Active Border Rule.** The accent marks focus and only focus. Selection of a mode, a year or a nav item is shown by inversion (ink fill, surface text), never by accent fill. Upstream's own brand colour follows the rule at the token: Tailwind's `--color-primary` (in `omarchy-world.css`) and the legacy `immich-primary` / `immich-dark-primary` pair (at the end of `app.css`'s theme, which defines them after its imports) are ink, so stock and @immich/ui markup (titles, icons, chips, meters, filled buttons) take ink and inversion with no per-component override, and text that upstream set white or black on such a fill takes the surface. The raw `--immich-ui-primary-*` ramp keeps the theme's accent for `--ws-accent` (2026-10-15).

**The Hairline Rule.** Separation is a 1px hairline or nothing. No tinted panels, no slabs, no second background tone for chrome.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Bricolage Grotesque Variable (wght + wdth + opsz axes, fallback Mona Sans)
**Body Font:** Mona Sans variable (self-hosted, wght 200 to 900, stretch 75 to 125%, `ss01` + `ss02` on)
**Label/Mono Font:** the desktop's monospace (`--omarchy-font-mono` from the font-set hook; fallback JetBrainsMono Nerd Font, then GoogleSansCode)

**Delivery.** Bricolage is loaded once, as Fontsource's standard axis set (wght, wdth, opsz) from `omarchy-world.css`. Until 2026-10-15 `gallery-theme.css` also imported the wght-only build under the same family name, the browser picked it, and nothing but the bar's heading ever condensed (Years numerals, the AI inbox headline and empty-state titles ran wide); that import and the unused DM Sans are gone. Mona Sans is preloaded (`app.html`, filled by `hooks.server.ts`); upstream's Google Sans (4.8 MB) and Google Sans Code are no longer preloaded, and nothing shows them.

**Character:** Condensed, heavy grotesque numerals set the time like a poster; a neutral variable sans does the work; the desktop's own terminal font carries every number that is data.

### Hierarchy
One size per role, as tokens in `omarchy-world.css` (`--ws-text-*`, in rem so one root size scales them all, for a TV); components use the token, never a literal size:
display `clamp(2.25rem, 1rem + 3vw, 4rem)` · title 1.25rem · heading 1.125rem · body 1rem · ui 0.875rem · meta 0.8125rem · data 0.75rem. Nothing a person reads is under 12px (the 11px micro size is retired into data).

**Upstream follows at the token** (2026-10-15): Tailwind's sizes are the roles, in a `@theme` block in `omarchy-world.css`, so a stock page reads as the same product as Photos. `text-xs` is meta (13px, +0.005em), `sm` ui, `base` body (line-height 1.55), `lg` heading (18px, 650), `xl` a title-sized line in Mona (20px), and `2xl` to `5xl` headlines and numerals in the display face at its condensed width (24, 30, 36, 48px; 750 then 800), so album and group titles, the Years and Months tiles, places and statistics speak the bar's voice at about upstream's sizes; `6xl` is the display role. Every h1-h4 is Mona at 650 (gallery-theme.css had sent them all to the display face, at 14px a 96pt poster design); a rule that wants the display face for a heading names it. `font-mono text-xs` keeps the data size; upstream's arbitrary 9-11.5px sizes are the data size; thin, extralight and light weights read as regular. On a dark ground body text runs at 420 and the small roles get a little more tracking and leading.

- **Display** (800, condensed `wdth 75`, `--ws-text-display`, line-height 0.85; numerals -0.035em, words -0.01em with 0.06-0.08em word spacing, since at the condensed width a tight setting runs words together): the year in view in the timeline header, page headlines (AI inbox, Command center), Years banners. With the glass chrome the timeline heading folds to title size in one slim glass row once the photos scroll (the space reserved above them keeps the open height, so nothing moves).
- **Display Month** (500, same size and width): the month name beside it, first letter capitalised. Weight contrast, not size, separates month from year.
- **Headline** (750, `wdth 80`, `clamp(1.75rem, 1.2rem + 1.6vw, 2.5rem)`, line-height 1.05, balanced wrap): empty-state titles.
- **Title** (650, 20px, -0.01em, display face): a page's name in its title bar.
- **Heading** (650, 18px): section and group names, inbox row titles.
- **Body** (400, 420 on dark, 16px, line-height 1.55, max 60ch): explanatory copy, empty-state bodies, day labels in the timeline (display face, tabular).
- **UI** (500-600, 14px): buttons, tabs, sidebar items, segmented options.
- **Meta** (400, 13px, muted): details and captions. Menus run a step smaller (12.5px) on the owner's wish.
- **Data** (600, 12px, tabular, monospace): counts ("59 items"), year pills, dates, EXIF, workspace numbers.

### Named Rules
**The Scale Contrast Rule.** Hierarchy comes from the jump between display numerals and 12px data, with title/heading/body as the only steps between; no new in-between sizes.

**The Data Is Mono Rule.** A number that is information (count, year, date, EXIF) is set in the desktop's monospace. A number that is a heading (the year in view, a day label) is set in the display face.

## Layout

**The gap module.** `--ws-gap` (fallback 10px) is Hyprland's inner gap times two, the space between two tiled windows. It is the justified-layout spacing between photos (read by `VirtualScrollManager` at layout time) and derives the header rhythm: strip pills step at 0.4 gap, the header sits 0.6 gap above and 0.8 gap below, and 2.4 gap splits the month heading from the year strip.

**Rows fill the width.** Days that fit on one line are pooled into shared rows, each re-laid at one height so it ends exactly at the viewport edge (photos shrink by at most 0.72x, grow up to 1.6x). Each run of such days is cut into rows as a whole, choosing the cuts that keep every row nearest the target height, the last included (2026-10-15: greedy pooling left each month's last row wherever its days ran out; the owner: "make it so that images align on the right side"). **Months share rows** (2026-10-15, the owner's yes): a month whose last row cannot fill even at 1.6x hands it to the next month, which cuts those days in with its own first ones; the handed-on days hang below their own month's box, exactly where the next month's box begins, so the row reads as one (`internal/layout-support`: a month box paints what overflows it and lets clicks through to its day sections; a month whose photos all go on that way has no height of its own). Sparse views fill the width now (Favorites: 33 of 48 rows short before, 2 of 58 after). Only where no next month can take them (the last month, or the next not yet loaded) does a short row widen its tiles, their edges cropped, by up to 1.35x, or stay short. A day too wide for one line takes the row to itself, its photos split into balanced lines each justified to full width. Rows sit on whole pixels: the height is rounded and the widths take the hair that rounding left, so every full row ends on the same pixel (flooring the height had left most rows a few pixels short). While any day's layout is deferred, the upstream packing is the fallback.

**Where months start** (2026-10-16, the owner chose "Quiet month marks" over "Moments with places" and "Both, by zoom"). With months sharing rows and day labels off, nothing in the grid said where one month ends and the next begins. Each layout notes where a month starts (`internal/layout-support`, `TimelineMonth.monthStart`: its first photo in grid order, that photo's row and the gaps around it, worked out once per layout and after the months that place its handed-on days), and the month draws two quiet things from it (`components/timeline/Month.svelte`), neither of which moves a tile: **the month mark** on its first photo (Photo Tile) and **a hairline in the gap** where it starts, mid-gap on whole pixels, in ink at 30% (`--ws-month-line`, a step firmer than the 13% of a divider on the ground, which all but vanished in a 10px gap beside bright photos). A month that opens a row has the line across the whole width in the gap above that row; a month that starts mid-row has it as a corner, "┌": down the gap before its first photo for that row's height, and along the gap above the row from there to the grid's edge, over its own photos only. The line follows the day sections when they glide (same delay, none while the timeline scrolls or zooms). At the tightest gaps (Compact, the smallest sizes: 1-2px) the line all but fills the gap, and the mark carries the boundary. The timeline's first month has neither: the bar names it. **Day labels on:** no marks and no lines, since every day's label already dates it (the month changes in the label right above its first photo).

**Shell.** One bar (`components/omarchy/OmarchyBar.svelte`, 56px, one hairline along its foot, the glass when chosen): menu and mark in a first column as wide as the sidebar; the page's own content in the middle (`lib/managers/bar-slot` `content`; Photos puts its month heading and the years there, and with filters on the month heading alone, and its Years and Months overviews their name ("Years", "Months") with the span of years they lay out in data type ("1977–2026"), where the bar had stood empty on a phone and held the wordmark alone wider; every other page its title row: back button, title in the bar heading face, description in mono, then, where the page shows a timeline of days (Favorites, an album, a person, Archive, Trash, a Space's photos), the month in view ending the title row behind a hairline as tall as the text, Photos' own heading in muted ink on the title's baseline (`TimelineMonthHeading`, following that timeline through `managers/timeline-heading`; not the map's photo panel; the row's share of the bar grows by its room), then its own buttons, the row fitting itself to the bar (below)); then the icons: a page's own tool first (`bar-slot` `tools`; Photos: the filter panel's reopen button), search as an icon that opens into the field over the middle, undo (only while there is something to take back: the recent actions, newest first, in a sheet like the View menu's; Ctrl+Z takes back the newest either way), the View menu (order, thumbnail size, day labels, expanded stacks, compact, then the theme: Follow Omarchy (the default) / Light / Dark, rounded corners, the inspector window (⇧I, see Inspector), then Cast), upload, notifications (on a phone in the account menu, below) and the account (28px, a ring at 38% ink with the initial in ink; a picture fills it; upstream's colour discs put the initial at 2.9:1 in dark themes). No second header row on the plain timeline. Sidebar shares the ground with a hairline edge. The filter rail starts collapsed; the timeline leads. **Theme and light/dark go together:** following Omarchy, the desktop picks light or dark (prefers-color-scheme, which Omarchy flips with its theme); a chosen theme brings its own; Light or Dark in the View menu holds one face until Follow Omarchy is picked again.

**The title row fits the bar** (`layouts/UserPageLayout.svelte`, `utils/page-bar-fit`; 2026-10-16). The title keeps its width and the page's own buttons tighten a step at a time until the row fits: the description goes (the month then takes its short name), then the button labels (icons keep their tooltips), then the wide-screen extras and the month, and at the last the buttons fold into one ⋮, the bar's own icon. Its sheet is the View menu's (one frame on the ground, the theme's corners, the popup shadow) and lists them as rows with their labels back, an icon alone showing its name; a menu or list of the page's own opens over it (a sort menu, Places' grouping select, an album's ⋮), a press on anything else or a choice in such a list closes it as a menu closes behind its item, and so do Escape (before the page hears it: an album's Escape goes back to Albums) and a press outside. The buttons are rendered once, inline or drawn as the sheet, never both, so bound fields and ids stay single. A page whose buttons are one control the size of the ⋮ (Shared's ⋮, Settings', a single filter) keeps it inline, since a ⋮ for it would take as much room and hide it a click deeper; one control wider than that (Places' grouping select, which keeps its label) folds. While the buttons share the bar the title row's share of it is capped (45%, 60% on a phone, more while the month shows), so they tighten before the name is cut, and a name cut short counts as not fitting, so the fold comes before the name is cut; nothing caps a page without buttons, a row at the fold, or a row with a segmented control beside the title (a Space's sections, which scroll sideways and give way first, down to about one section, keeping the one on screen in view). The Map's filter toggle is the page's own tool after the title like every page's (it stood before the title on a phone and over "Map" on the 4K screen). Upstream's Dropdown says it opens a list (`aria-haspopup`, `aria-expanded`), which also keeps the sheet open under it.

**On a phone** (below 640px) the bar's icons are search, View and the account: notifications move into the account menu (a row with the unread count in data type after the accent's dot; the panel opens from the account in the menu's place), and the bar's dot sits on the avatar's ring while something is unread. Wider bars keep the bell. A page that heads its body with its name as a poster (the AI inbox, Command center, Chat, Ask; `UserPageLayout`'s `titleInBody`) leaves the name to the poster on a wider screen, where the bar shows the wordmark; on a phone the bar carries the name and the poster steps aside, still the page's heading for a screen reader, its count and controls staying.

**One gutter.** Every page starts on the same line, 0.5rem in from the sidebar: the bar's page part (a title, a back arrow, Photos' month heading), the photo grids (no extra inset of their own) and the text columns (Shared, Settings, the AI inbox, Utilities; no side padding of their own). The bar's own icons stand behind a full-height hairline with 0.5rem on each side, apart from the page's tools (2026-10-15; the owner saw "spacing inconsistency at the command bar").

**Responsive.** Below 768px: header stacks, day labels shorten to weekday + date, the grouping control floats at the bottom, the filters open as a sheet over the photos (Filter Rail), and Ctrl+K's field takes its short hints, the four scopes alone ("@ people, # tags, / albums, > pages") and Ask's lead without its example ("Ask about your photos"): a phone's field holds about 314px of hint, and the full ones need 316 to 530 (2026-10-16). Below 640px the bar keeps the menu button without the mark, upload waits for a wider screen (a phone uploads through the app; Import stays in the sidebar), and notifications move into the account menu (Shell). On coarse pointers, tiles at least 200px wide show their place caption permanently.

## Elevation & Depth

Flat on the ground; depth only for what floats above it, like a Hyprland popup. Upstream `shadow-sm/md/lg` utilities are zeroed. Tiles have no shadow; their edge is an inset hairline.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Popup** (`box-shadow: 0 12px 32px -8px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.28)` plus a 1px hairline border): menus, dialogs, listboxes, and the floating (mobile) grouping control.

### Named Rules
**The Floats Only Rule.** A shadow means the element floats over the ground and can be dismissed. Anything that lives on the ground is flat.

## Shapes

Corners come from the desktop: everything (tiles, menus, modals, toasts, fields, buttons, pills) uses the window radius `--ws-radius` exactly (Hyprland `decoration:rounding`; fallback 0px, square). Never add an offset to it: the owner rejected rounded controls on the square desktop (2026-10-12). A panel has one frame; separators run edge to edge. The last rules that added +4..10px were brought back to the token on 2026-10-13. Borders take the desktop's `--ws-border` (`general:border_size`, minimum 1px; fallback 2px) for focus, 1px for hairlines. Upstream's markup follows at the token: the whole Tailwind radius scale (`--radius-xs` to `--radius-4xl`) is `var(--ws-radius)` in `omarchy-world.css`, so stock cards, bars, fields and buttons square with the desktop; only `rounded-full` stays round, for faces and avatars. Photo tiles take the radius exactly at every size (the zoom-following ~6px of 2026-10-10 went on 2026-10-15). **Rounded corners** (the View menu's switch, per browser, 2026-10-16, `lib/theme/corners.svelte.ts`) is the one exception, and the owner's to choose: it puts `--ws-radius` at 8px whatever the theme (`:root[data-corners='rounded']`, set before the first paint by `app.html`), and the tiles on `--tile-radius`, which follows the zoom (~2px at the smallest size, 6px at the default, 8px for big tiles, halved in Compact) so the gaps between photos stay lines; faces stay round either way. Upstream's round controls (@immich/ui `shape="round"` icon buttons and buttons, control bars, switches, pill links) take it too; whatever holds a picture keeps its circle, so faces stay round, as do avatars, dots and spinners, which are not controls. The layer's own pills (the viewer's toolbar, stack badges, the drag count, Curator's tool pills and buttons, the compare handle) went square the same day; a brush ring, a spinner and a face stay round.

Tile focus is drawn **inset** (an inset box-shadow on a pseudo-element), because day sections use layout/paint containment and an outset border would clip at section edges.

## Motion

Things move the way the owner's desktop moves them. The theme hook reads Hyprland's animations (`hyprctl animations`) into motion tokens beside the geometry, so a window's animation on the desktop is a photo's animation here, and turning an animation off there turns it off here (0ms). `omarchy-world.css` carries the owner's values as fallbacks; `lib/utils/desktop-motion.ts` reads the same tokens for motion written in script.

- **Arriving: no motion, speed instead.** Photos, albums, folders and months are simply there when they come into view or a page opens. Two tries at an arrival animation were dropped on 2026-10-15: a window-style pop from the tile's first pixel played out unseen at the screen's edge, and one held until a fifth of the tile was in left blank strips and popped while the tile moved (the owner: "still animates a bit weird", then "or should we skip those animation and focus on speed"). Speed is the arrival now: the timeline makes tiles, and fetches their thumbnails, about a screen above and two below the view (`lib/utils/tunables.ts`, 1300px / 2600px; upstream 500px each), and Page Up / Page Down jump at once (`lib/utils/page-keys.ts`), so presses in a row, or the key held down, page as fast as they come onto photos already loaded.
- **Close** (`--ws-close-*` from windowsOut; popin 92%, 350ms): a photo that leaves (trash, archive, To do, Villa, QR) shrinks to the popin scale as it fades.
- **Re-tile** (`--ws-move-*` from windowsMove; 400ms on the snap curve `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`): the photos left behind glide into their new places, as windows do when one closes.
- **Workspace** (`--ws-workspace-*` from workspaces; slidefade 20%, 500ms snap): switching years (see Time Strip).
- **Favourite:** a heart given while the photo is on screen pops in from 40% on the open curve (0.65 of the open time); a heart already there when the tile is made just sits there.
- **Never in the way:** the timeline's own suspension (scrolling, zoom, relayout) stops close and re-tile, so nothing moves while the view does.
- **Reduced motion:** a leaving photo only fades (150ms at most) and the rest re-tile at once; years cut.

## Components

### Photo Tile (signature)
Every photo is a window.
- **Shape:** window radius (`--ws-radius`) on the container, image and focus layer.
- **Rest:** inset 1px hairline.
- **Focus / Hover:** inset `--ws-border` in the theme accent (hover counts as focus, like focus-follows-mouse); default outline suppressed. 140ms `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`; reduced motion: no transition.
- **Date and place caption:** the photo's date in data type (weekday, day and month, and the year unless it is this year's: months share rows, and the bar names the month at the top of the view, not the one pointed at), then city and country, on a bottom black scrim, white 12px 600, fades in with focus (140ms); indented past the favourite star. With day labels on, the place alone. Permanent on coarse pointers for tiles 200px and wider. The dates come from one `Intl.DateTimeFormat` kept per locale and style (`utils/timeline-dates`): building one per tile cost ~26µs in Chromium against under 1µs to format, and a fast scrub makes about a hundred tiles a frame.
- **Month mark** (the month's first photo in the grid; see Layout, Where months start): the locale's short month in capitals, in data type ("SEP"), with the year in the locale's order where the year changes from the month before ("DEC 2025", "2025年12月"), on strong glass with white text at the top-left, 0.5rem in, at most half the tile wide (the year goes under the month on a narrow tile), over the tile's badges; closer to the corner on tiles under 7.5rem (0.25rem, clear of a video's time, and a stack's badge beside it keeps its count without the icon) and hugging it under 5rem, where the tile is too narrow for the mark and anything at its other top corner (a phone's portrait videos ran "SEP" into the play icon, 2026-10-16): there the mark keeps the top edge to itself, and what sits at that corner (a video's or live photo's play icon, a GIF's or 360° photo's mark, a photo stack's badge; `.ws-tile-corner`) gives way on that one tile. - **What it shows, down the portrait screen:** the photo whole on the ground at preview size (never the original), its stage as tall as the photo at the window's width and at most 62% of the window, so the details sit right under a wide photo too; a video shows its poster and plays on demand. Then the date in the bar heading's voice at the display role (the condensed face, the month 500, the year 750, in the reader's order), the weekday and time on the camera's own clock with its zone in data type, as the Info panel says it, the place, and a small map without controls (Map.svelte). Then in two columns as the width allows: the named people as round faces with their names and how many more have none, the albums it is in (cover, name, count and years), its description (Curator's caption, when Curator wrote it, marked written by AI), Curator's story, and the details (camera, lens, exposure, file name, megapixels, pixels, size). Section names in muted meta, numbers in data type; a hairline under the stage and one above the columns. Nothing pointed at yet: one quiet line on the gutter, "Point at a photo in Gallery". A preview Gallery cannot give (videos without thumbnails) is a quiet placeholder. Read-only.
- **Structural departure:** a route outside the user pages' shell (`routes/inspector`: no bar, no sidebar, no drop to upload) with its own load (login as any page); `Thumbnail` tells the feed what is pointed at; the user pages' layout mounts `components/inspector/InspectorFeed.svelte` (⇧I, the viewer and the selection); the View menu has its row; `?asset=` shows one photo without a main window.

### Memories (the cards and the film)
The owner's choices for "Memories worth opening" (2026-10-16): titles of "facts, place and time", and "Full-screen film" over "Framed and calm".

**The cards** (`/memories` and Explore; `components/memories/MemoryCard.svelte`): a memory is a tile like a photo's. Its cover (Curator's pick of its best photo, else its first) lies under the caption scrim, its title in the display face at title size (750, condensed, balanced) and its line under it in data type; focus and the pointer draw the accent's inset `--ws-border`, as on a photo.
- **A title of facts** (`utils/memory-title`, through `memoryLaneTitle`, so every card, the film and the next-memory offer agree): the places worth naming (at most two, most photos first, joined with the locale's own "and"), else the named people (at most three), then " · " and when: one month ("Siem Reap · October 2025"), months within a year ("March – May 2021"; January to December is the year), close months over a New Year ("December 2024 – January 2025"), else the years ("2019–2021"); with neither places nor people, the time alone. Month names, ranges and "and" come from Intl in the reader's locale. Curator's facts say it best (`GET /api/memories/facts`: the places and people, the photos' span, the count the memory stands for, the cover and the faces' centres; `managers/memory-facts`, each memory once per page load, in batches, for Curator's owner only); without them a memory says it itself: its rule's city or place label (a country), its people, its month or year, else its photos' dates. A birthday keeps "Alex's birthday" (its `year` is the birth year), and a memory with nothing to go on its old title.
- **The line** is what the card said before under its title, the rule's own ("1 year ago · 192 photos over 4 days", "26 favorites"); an on-this-day card's line says how long ago, which was its title ("1 year ago"); people together and a person's throwback count their photos alone, the month being in the title.
- A card waits for Curator's answer (2 s at most) before it shows its cover and title, so neither changes under the reader's eyes; a family account waits for nothing.

**The film** (`/memories/[id]`; `components/memories/film/MemoryFilm.svelte`): full screen over the shell like the viewer, on black.
- **The plan** (`film-plan.ts`, `buildFilmPlan`): everything the film does is decided before it plays, from the memory's photos, the frame's shape and Curator's facts and songs, as plain JSON data, so Curator's "Save as video" can render the same film from it: the shots (start, time, crossfade, fit, the camera at start and end), the texts (the title card, the story's sentences) and the songs (start, end, fades). A photo has the Memories duration preference (5 s), the title card 5 s, a crossfade 1.4 s (the first photo 1.2 s out of black), a video at most 8 s; when the first song's tempo is known, every cut falls on its beat (a skip keeps the cuts).
- **The title card:** Curator's cover opens the film with the title over it (a cover that is the first photo gets the title card as part of its own shot; any other is shown again in its place). The name (the places or people, else the time) in the display face, 800, condensed, up to 6rem, and under it the time and the count in data type, uppercase and spaced ("OCTOBER 2025 · 192 PHOTOS"; the count the memory stands for, a trip's whole), bottom-left on the caption scrim. It comes up half a second in (a 14px rise and a fade) and goes as the next photo comes.
- **The photos:** a photo fills the frame when a crop keeps at least 60% of it (a phone's 3:4 photos fill a phone held upright; a square one on the AORUS does not) and drifts, a slow zoom between 1.04 and 1.12 toward the faces' centre (Curator's `focus`) or out from it, in and out by turns; without faces, on the middle with a small pan. One a crop would ruin is shown whole on its own colours: its thumbhash, a little richer and dimmed, scaled up behind it under a vignette (the viewer's `.ws-ambient` idea, but no CSS blur: under a moving photo a blur is worked out again every frame and took two thirds of the frame rate at 2560×1440), breathing between 1 and 1.035. A video plays muted for up to 8 s and holds still while the music carries on.
- **The story** (Curator's, as before, for its owner) is one quiet line at the bottom-left, the body face at 450, 1.06 to 1.63rem with the screen, 44ch at most, a sentence at a time across the photos after the title card: a long sentence over more photos than a short one, two sentences to a photo when there are more of them than photos. Never a box. The ⋮ hides it and brings it back (remembered per browser).
- **The controls** sit on the viewer's thin glass: at the top-left the close and the memory's title in the bar heading face, with the date and place of the photo on screen in data type (a phone gives the date and place two lines and leaves the name to the title card); at the top-right the memory's heart, View in timeline and ⋮ (Remove memory, Remove photo from this memory, which takes the photo on screen: the address is put on it first; the film pauses while it is open); at the bottom-right ♪ artist – title (a press: the next song), the photo before, play and pause, the next, where the film is in data type ("3/20"), mute and All photos. Keys: Space pauses, ← and → step a photo, M mutes, N skips to the next song, Escape closes; with Ctrl, Alt or Meta held, in a field or in a menu, a key is left alone. The controls fade after 3 s and come back with the pointer, a touch or any key; paused, at the end, under the pointer, with a menu open or with keyboard focus in them, they stay. A tap shows or hides them; a swipe steps a photo on or back. Hidden, their glass stops blurring the photo, the buttons staying where a screen reader and Tab find them.
- **Music** (Curator's owner only; `film-music.svelte.ts`): Curator's songs from the owner's own library (`GET /api/memories/{id}/music?n=3`), played from its origin on `<audio>` with the session cookie (no crossorigin). The first starts with the film, fading in over 1.5 s; the next follows when one ends, or when skipped to (the one before fading out under it); the music fades out over the film's last 4 s and in 0.4 s on a pause. Opening a memory from its card is a press, so the sound starts with it; a link opened cold starts silent, with "Turn on sound" as a white pill at the bottom-right until it is pressed. Without songs (another account, Curator silent, nothing that fits) the film is silent, with no ♪ and no mute. iOS keeps an `<audio>` at full volume, so there the music starts and stops without its fades.
- **Save as video** (the ⋮, Curator's owner, once Curator lists `film.render`): the film hands Curator its plan, laid out for a 16:9 frame from what it was planned from (cover, faces, a photo's time), its songs from the one playing with the cuts on that song's beat, and Curator draws the same film into an MP4 (`curator/film.py`: 1080p at 30 fps, the drift, crossfades, title card, story, scrim and song as the browser plays them, the web's own faces). A square panel in the bottom-left corner's stack, in the frame of the videos for sharing and between the photo books and them (`components/omarchy/FilmVideoPanel.svelte`), says how far it has got (waiting, then the share of its frames over the 2px line), then its size with a download, or that it failed with a retry, or that a film over five minutes cannot be saved yet; the browser saves "<title>.mp4" by itself once it is ready. While the film is up, the corner's panels wait at the top-left under the close and the title instead, so they never sit on the film's words.
- **The end:** the last photo dims under the scrim, the name comes back, and three quiet offers on strong glass: Start over, the next memory (a small photo of it and its title), All photos.
- **All photos** puts the film away (it pauses, and Play brings it back where it was) for the memory's photos as a grid on the dark ground, under a slim bar: the close, the title in the bar heading face with the count in data type, and Play inverted (ink fill). A photo opens in the viewer; back from it, the film moves to that photo.
- **Reduced motion:** no drift (the crop still toward the faces), plain 0.8 s fades, the title without its rise.
- **Widths:** the frame is the window (a phone upright or on its side, the Dell's portrait, the AORUS); while the film is up the page goes under a phone's notch (`viewport-fit=cover`, set and taken back) and the controls keep to the safe areas. On a phone the words take the width and the controls a row of their own under them, 44px targets.
- **Performance:** two layers crossfade, the photo on screen and the next one, decoded under it while the first plays (the one after is fetched into the cache, at preview size; never the original), so a memory of any length is two photos and about 80 elements. The drift and the fades are Web Animations of transform and opacity, run by the compositor; the script wakes only at the plan's next change (a timer, no frame loop) and waits for a photo that is not decoded yet. Measured in Chromium at 2560×1440: 60 fps through 15 s of playback with crossfades, the worst frame 17 ms, no long task. The address is not moved along per photo (a navigation every few seconds); the photo it names starts the film.
- **Structural departure:** the memory route (`MemoryViewer.svelte`) renders the film and All photos; upstream's framed player (a progress bar of segments, the memories before and after at its sides, `MemoryPhotoViewer` and `MemoryVideoViewer`) and the story's box (`curator/MemoryStory.svelte`) are gone.

### Room (the archive across the room)
`/room` (`components/room/RoomPlayer.svelte`, 2026-10-18, the owner's "Room mode": TV-first, any screen; today the AORUS when they sit back, later a TV's browser) plays the films of Memories across the room, full screen without the shell (no sidebar, no bar; the window goes full screen too, within the press that opened Room, and comes out with Room; F and a button in the top bar take it in and out in place, and the browser's own way out, its Escape, leaves Room with it, so one Escape leaves as in the film), on black. What Curator makes on request (the corner panels) stays off it.
- **What it plays** (`room-reels.ts`, `room-source.ts`): plain `/room` is a chain of films, today's memories (the day's, a birthday first, for someone who keeps memories on) and then the albums Curator keeps for the TV once accepted, found by the names Curator's own Kiosk job looks for ("Best of <year>", "<Month> across the years", "Growing up · <name>", "On the TV"; none yet, 17 wait in the AI inbox) and played On the TV first, then this month across the years, the years' best from the newest, growing up, the other months from this one on; then round again. `?album=` plays that album, its photos in the order they were taken; `?year=` plays the year's favourites spread through its months (48 at most: each month in turn, the busiest first, taken evenly through it), or, with fewer than 12 favourites, a sample of 48 by month with the favourites in it (photos only), and its title card says which: "36 favorites", "48 of 212 favorites", "A sample of 48 from 3,412 photos" (the year counted as the Years overview counts it). A lone album or year plays again. Only what Photos shows plays: every search names the timeline's visibility, so archived photos (To do, Villa, QR's bank slips), hidden ones and the Locked Folder stay out, an unlocked session included. A film with nothing to play leaves the chain.
- **The same film, dressed for three metres** (`MemoryFilm`'s `dress="room"`): its sizes follow the screen's shorter side, so a 1080p TV, the AORUS and a 4K TV read alike: the title about 150px at 1080p (14vmin), the title card's line and the caption 32px (3vmin), the story 42px, controls 65px with 35px icons, and the margins in a TV's title-safe area (4.8vmin). The **caption** at the foot gives the photo on screen in words: who is in it (its named people, three and then how many more), where (city, country) and when (its day), "Anna and Ben · Siem Reap, Cambodia · October 16, 2025", from the photo's details (asked once, the next two ahead). It comes with the photo after the title card's and sits on the line under the story; the date and place leave the top bar for it, which keeps the close, the title and, at its end, Up next (the next film's title; a press goes there) and full screen. No heart, no ⋮, no All photos and no Save as video: nothing in Room changes anything. The pointer is hidden until the mouse moves and again once it rests (3 s).
- **One after another** (`onNextFilm`, `onPreviousFilm`): a film's end hands on instead of offering Start over: the picture and the music fade to black (650ms; 150ms with reduced motion) and the next film's title card comes up out of black.
- **Keys and a remote:** Space or a remote's OK (Enter) pauses and plays (Enter on a control reached with the keys presses it), ← and → step a photo, ↑ and ↓ go to the film before and after, M mutes, N skips to the next song, F full screen, Escape or a TV's Back (GoBack, BrowserBack, XF86Back) leaves, and a remote's or a keyboard's media keys play, pause and skip films (while a song plays the browser hands them to the page's media session, which Room takes, or they would pause the song alone). The controls come and go as in the film. Where a link opened cold held the sound back, the first key starts it, and OK then does only that.
- **Music** as in the films: Curator's songs for a memory, its owner's only. A family account's Room is silent, and so are albums and years: Curator picks songs for memories only.
- **Awake** (`room-awake.ts`): while Room plays, loads or hands on, a screen wake lock (the Wake Lock API, taken again when the page comes back); paused, it lets go. Where the browser has no such API (a TV's browser over plain http) or refuses the lock, a black 32px video, silent and out of sight, loops instead.
- **Nothing to play:** on black, where the title card stands: "Nothing to play today", why (and, for the owner, that Curator's albums for the TV wait to be accepted, with Review the albums in the AI inbox), Play <last year> in white, which takes the focus so a remote's OK presses it (the arrows move between the choices), and Close. An album or a year with nothing to play, and Gallery not answering (with Retry), say so the same way. While the first film loads, the room is black, and one quiet line says what it waits for once the wait goes past 0.7 s.
- **Leaving:** back where Room was opened from; opened cold, to the album, the year in Photos, or Photos.
- **Ways in:** Room in the sidebar after Memories (every account) and in Ctrl+K; Room in the Memories page's bar (today's films); Play in Room in an album's bar after Slideshow (in its ⋮ on a narrow bar; never for To do, Villa or QR); and Room on strong glass at the foot of each year's card in Photos' Years overview, opposite the year (the card still opens its months).
- **Structural departure:** a route of its own, `(user)/room`. `MemoryFilm` takes any run of photos with an id (`Pick<MemoryResponseDto, 'id' | 'assets'>`), a `dress`, a `caption`, the chain's `onNextFilm`/`onPreviousFilm`, a bindable `paused` and `control()`, and All photos is optional; `Timeline` and `TimelineRepresentativeBuckets` take a `bucketAction` snippet for a page's own action on a period's card; the memory page and Room word a memory's film with one helper (`memoryFilmWords`).

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** read every colour from the runtime roles (`--immich-ui-light`, `--immich-ui-dark`, `--immich-ui-muted`, `--ws-accent`, `--ws-hairline`) and keep the fallbacks as fallbacks.
- **Do** space tiles and derive header rhythm from `--ws-gap`; size tile corners and focus borders from `--ws-radius` and `--ws-border`.
- **Do** show the active/selected state by inversion (ink fill, surface text).
- **Do** set counts, years-as-data, dates and EXIF in the desktop monospace at 12px.
- **Do** give every motion a reduced-motion cut (no transition, no slide).
- **Do** style upstream markup through stable hooks and `ws-` classes so the layer rebases cleanly.

### Don't:
- **Don't** hard-code a theme's hex values, or add a brand colour of the layer's own.
- **Don't** use the accent as a fill for selected modes, nav items or years; it is the focus border.
- **Don't** put chrome on a second background tone or a rounded card slab; split the one ground with a hairline.
- **Don't** shadow anything that sits on the ground (tiles, rows, headers, the sidebar).
- **Don't** draw tile focus outset; it clips inside contained day sections.
