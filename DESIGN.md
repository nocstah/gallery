# Design System: Noodle Gallery (local Omarchy layer)

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Tiled Workspace"**

The archive is a workspace you live in, drawn in the grammar of the owner's own Omarchy/Hyprland desktop. Years are workspaces you switch between; photos are windows tiled on one even gap; the theme accent is the active border and lights only what has focus. The chrome is the same ground as the content, split by 1px hairlines, so the photos stay the brightest thing on screen.

This layer is a citizen of the desktop, not a brand. **Colour is not owned here.** The palette (`--immich-ui-light`, `--immich-ui-dark`, `--immich-ui-muted`, `--immich-ui-gray`, `--immich-ui-default-border`, the `--immich-ui-primary-*` ramp, the `--color-gray-*` ramp, `--omarchy-accent`, `--omarchy-on-accent`) and the desktop geometry and font (`--ws-gap`, `--ws-radius`, `--ws-border`, `--omarchy-font-mono`) arrive at runtime as admin Custom CSS that `~/.local/bin/immich-omarchy-theme` generates from the active Omarchy theme and Hyprland config (run by the theme-set and font-set hooks). `web/src/styles/omarchy-world.css` owns type, geometry rules, material, components, layout and motion, and only reads those tokens. Every theme colour and geometry value in the frontmatter is the build's **fallback** (the light face of `gallery-theme.css`, and the `:root` defaults in `omarchy-world.css`), shown when the hook has not run. Never hard-code a value from one theme. The standalone public memory viewer has its own fixed neutral roles (`public-memory-*`), without the owner's desktop hook; its scoped exception is described under Colors and Memories.

The layer began visual only: it styles upstream markup through stable hooks (`data-testid`, `data-thumbnail-focus-container`, ids) and `ws-` classes added to upstream components. It is now a deliberate fork (PRODUCT.md, Change scope) that also owns screens and flows: the Time Strip, the sidebar's Album Tree, its Pinned albums and photos carried to it, the Explorer of Tags and Folders, the Shared feed and the Share sheet, the special albums, Bank slips, the tool pages (Duplicates, Manage location, Large files, Workflows, Import), Album folders, the Space page, Photo books, the Adjust panel, the Memories cards and film, Room, and Curator's inbox and panels, each described under Components. A redesign may restructure a screen; every structural departure from upstream gets its entry under Components when it lands, so a merge of an upstream release knows what is ours.

**Key Characteristics:**
- One ground: the theme surface everywhere, divided by hairlines, never two-tone slabs.
- One gap module (`--ws-gap`, Hyprland `gaps_in` x2) spaces the tiles and derives the header rhythm.
- Tiles take the desktop's window radius and border width.
- Active state is inversion (ink fill, surface text), like the Omarchy bar's active workspace; the accent is reserved for focus.
- Scale contrast carries hierarchy: condensed heavy numerals at display size against 12px monospace data.
- Depth only on what floats (menus, dialogs, the floating grouping control).

## Colors

The palette is the active Omarchy theme, read at runtime; this system defines roles, not hues.

### Primary
- **Theme Accent** (`--ws-accent` = `var(--omarchy-accent, var(--immich-ui-primary-500))`; fallback in frontmatter `accent`): the active border. Used only for focus and response: the focused tile's border, `:focus-visible` outlines, text selection (26% mix), native `accent-color`, the hover underline of text links, and a 15% tint into the empty-state button on hover. Runtime source: the theme's accent from `colors.toml`, contrast-pushed by the hook.

### Neutral
- **Theme Surface** (`--immich-ui-light`; fallback `surface`): the single ground for page, top bar, sidebar, filter rail and floating controls. Also the text colour on inverted (active) elements.
- **Theme Ink** (`--immich-ui-dark`; fallback `ink`): primary text, display numerals, and the fill of every active/selected element.
- **Muted Ink** (`--immich-ui-muted`; fallback `muted`): secondary text: counts, inactive year pills, inactive segmented options, empty-state body. The hook pushes it to at least 4.6:1 against the surface.
- **Hairline** (`--ws-hairline` = ink at 13% in OKLab; fallback `hairline`): every divider, the tile's inactive border, control outlines, menu and dialog borders.
- **Glass** (`--ws-glass`, `--ws-glass-strong`, `--ws-glass-hover`, `--ws-glass-edge`): smoked glass on the photo, fixed like the scrim: thin for bars and pills, strong for chips and cards that hold text, and a step denser for a button on the photo while it is pointed at (the Years overview's Room, Duplicates' view; 2026-10-18, where 0.8 and 0.82 had each been written out).
- **Caption Scrim / Caption Ink** (`caption-scrim`, `caption-ink`): fixed colours, and only on the photo itself: the bottom gradient and white text of a caption on a photo (a tile's place caption, Places' tiles, the Years and Months tiles, Explore's week). The gradient holds a floor of black behind every word, the most its type needs for WCAG AA over a white wall, and eases out in a band above the words: 55% behind small type (4.5:1), 48% behind a numeral at the large size (3:1). It had been black 55% at the foot fading to nothing, which left the words of the place captions on a quarter of that (2026-10-18). They sit on imagery, not on the theme. The scrim a phone's filter sheet lays over the photos beside it (black at 50%, as the map's drawer has) is the same kind of thing: a dim on imagery.
- **QR Black / QR White**: the other fixed pair, a shared link's QR code (`components/omarchy/ShareLinkPanel.svelte`), black on a white quiet zone whatever the theme: a phone's camera needs the contrast and the quiet zone, and a dark theme would run into the code's edge. Like the caption, it is something to read off the screen, not chrome.

- **Public Memory Neutrals** (`public-memory-ground`, `public-memory-ink`, `public-memory-hover`, `public-memory-boundary`; `web/memory-player/style.css`): the standalone recipient viewer's fixed ground, foreground and inverted action fill, action hover, and field/scrollbar boundaries. These neutrals keep the cover, film and grid on one quiet ground outside the owner's themed shell; the private sharing sheet uses the theme roles above.

### Danger
- **Danger** (`--ws-danger`, `omarchy-world.css`; 2026-10-18): what trashes or deletes, as words on the ground (a menu's Delete, an import's failed count, an expired link) or as a fill under the surface's text (Duplicates' and Bank slips' Move to Trash). The hook gives no red, so the role is @immich/ui's danger red taken toward the theme's own ink (the light face's deep step at 80%, the dark face's lighter step at 65%), as far as holds AA on the theme's ground and on a menu's glass over a mid-grey photo: at least 4.6:1 over all of the owner's themes, in both faces (sakura-ancient: 7.9:1 light, 8.8:1 dark). A menu's destructive row inverts in it when pointed at (the danger fill, the words and the hint in the surface's colour), the same pair as at rest; a red tint under red words had taken the row to 2.2:1. A fill hovered goes further toward the ink, which only adds contrast. Before, the menus drew a fixed red (`oklch(0.66 0.19 25)`, 2.6:1 on the light ground) and Bank slips a fixed `#c62828` (3.1:1 under the dark face's text).

### Named Rules
**The Desktop Decides Rule.** No colour literal enters the build except the on-photo scrims (the caption's, a sheet's over the photos), a QR code's black on white and the standalone public memory viewer's fixed neutral roles (`public-memory-*`). New authenticated surfaces take roles (`--immich-ui-*`, `--ws-accent`, `--ws-hairline`), never hex values from the theme you happen to be looking at. The public viewer's exception does not change the owner's theme or the private sharing sheet.

**The Active Border Rule.** The accent marks focus and only focus. Selection of a mode, a year or a nav item is shown by inversion (ink fill, surface text), never by accent fill. Upstream's own brand colour follows the rule at the token: Tailwind's `--color-primary` (in `omarchy-world.css`) and the legacy `immich-primary` / `immich-dark-primary` pair (at the end of `app.css`'s theme, which defines them after its imports) are ink, so stock and @immich/ui markup (titles, icons, chips, meters, filled buttons) take ink and inversion with no per-component override, and text that upstream set white or black on such a fill takes the surface. The raw `--immich-ui-primary-*` ramp keeps the theme's accent for `--ws-accent` (2026-10-15).

**The Hairline Rule.** Separation is a 1px hairline or nothing. No tinted panels, no slabs, no second background tone for chrome.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Bricolage Grotesque Variable (wght + wdth + opsz axes, fallback Mona Sans)
**Body Font:** Mona Sans variable (self-hosted, wght 200 to 900, stretch 75 to 125%, `ss01` + `ss02` on)
**Label/Mono Font:** the desktop's monospace (`--omarchy-font-mono` from the font-set hook; fallback JetBrainsMono Nerd Font, then GoogleSansCode)

**Delivery.** Bricolage is loaded once, as Fontsource's standard axis set (wght, wdth, opsz) from `omarchy-world.css`. Until 2026-10-15 `gallery-theme.css` also imported the wght-only build under the same family name, the browser picked it, and nothing but the bar's heading ever condensed (Years numerals, the AI inbox headline and empty-state titles ran wide); that import and the unused DM Sans are gone. Mona Sans is preloaded (`app.html`, filled by `hooks.server.ts`); upstream's Google Sans (4.8 MB) and Google Sans Code are no longer preloaded by the authenticated shell. The standalone public memory viewer loads Mona Sans, Bricolage Grotesque Variable and GoogleSansCode from the same self-hosted font assets, using GoogleSansCode for data without the desktop font hook.

**Character:** Condensed, heavy grotesque numerals set the time like a poster; a neutral variable sans does the work; the desktop's own terminal font carries every number that is data.

### Hierarchy
One size per role, as tokens in `omarchy-world.css` (`--ws-text-*`, in rem so one root size scales them all, for a TV); components use the token, never a literal size:
display `clamp(2.25rem, 1rem + 3vw, 4rem)` · title 1.25rem · heading 1.125rem · body 1rem · ui 0.875rem · meta 0.8125rem · data 0.75rem. Nothing a person reads is under 12px (the 11px micro size is retired into data).

**Upstream follows at the token** (2026-10-15): Tailwind's sizes are the roles, in a `@theme` block in `omarchy-world.css`, so a stock page reads as the same product as Photos. `text-xs` is meta (13px, +0.005em), `sm` ui, `base` body (line-height 1.55), `lg` heading (18px, 650), `xl` a title-sized line in Mona (20px), and `2xl` to `5xl` headlines and numerals in the display face at its condensed width (24, 30, 36, 48px; 750 then 800), so album and group titles, the Years and Months tiles, places and statistics speak the bar's voice at about upstream's sizes; `6xl` is the display role. Every h1-h4 is Mona at 650 (gallery-theme.css had sent them all to the display face, at 14px a 96pt poster design); a rule that wants the display face for a heading names it. `font-mono text-xs` keeps the data size; upstream's arbitrary 9-11.5px sizes are the data size; thin, extralight and light weights read as regular. On a dark ground body text runs at 420 and the small roles get a little more tracking and leading.

- **Display** (800, condensed `wdth 75`, `--ws-text-display`, line-height 0.85; numerals -0.035em, words -0.01em with 0.06-0.08em word spacing, since at the condensed width a tight setting runs words together): the year in view in the timeline header, page headlines (AI inbox, Command center), Years banners. With the glass chrome the timeline heading folds to title size in one slim glass row once the photos scroll (the space reserved above them keeps the open height, so nothing moves).
- **Display Month** (500, same size and width): the month name beside it, first letter capitalised. Weight contrast, not size, separates month from year.
- **Headline** (750, `wdth 80`, `clamp(1.75rem, 1.2rem + 1.6vw, 2.5rem)`, line-height 1.05, balanced wrap): empty-state titles.
- **Title** (650, 20px, -0.01em, display face): a page's name in its title bar.
- **Heading** (650, 18px): section and group names, inbox row titles.
- **Body** (400, 420 on dark, 16px, line-height 1.55, max 60ch): explanatory copy, empty-state bodies, day labels in the timeline (display face, tabular).
- **UI** (500-600, 14px): buttons, tabs, sidebar items, segmented options.
- **Meta** (400, 13px, muted): details and captions. Menus run a step smaller (12.5px) on the owner's wish.
- **Data** (600, 12px, tabular, monospace): counts ("59 items"), year pills, dates, EXIF, workspace numbers.

**Sizes that follow their surface** (2026-10-18). A surface that is a frame of its own sets its own scale, and its words follow it between two sizes instead of taking one role: the film's title card (up to 6rem with the screen) and its story (1.06 to 1.63rem), Room's words (the screen's shorter side, for three metres), and the Years and Months overviews' tile captions (a year's numeral 24% of its tile from 1rem to 3.5rem, a month's name 20% from 1rem to 2.75rem; see Years and Months overviews). These are the only fluid sizes. Everything else on such a surface takes its role: the film's top bar names the memory at the bar heading size (`--ws-text-bar`; it had run 18 to 24px with the window), its end names it again at display size (`--ws-text-display`; 28 to 52px before), and the selection bar counts at the bar heading size of the month heading it stands in for (25.6px before).

### Named Rules
**The Scale Contrast Rule.** Hierarchy comes from the jump between display numerals and 12px data, with title/heading/body as the only steps between; no new in-between sizes.

**The Data Is Mono Rule.** A number that is information (count, year, date, EXIF) is set in the desktop's monospace. A number that is a heading (the year in view, a day label) is set in the display face.

## Layout

**The gap module.** `--ws-gap` (fallback 10px) is Hyprland's inner gap times two, the space between two tiled windows. It is the justified-layout spacing between photos (read by `VirtualScrollManager` at layout time) and derives the header rhythm: strip pills step at 0.4 gap, the header sits 0.6 gap above and 0.8 gap below, and 2.4 gap splits the month heading from the year strip.

**Rows fill the width.** A month's photos are cut into rows, each re-laid at one height so it ends exactly at the viewport edge (photos shrink by at most 0.72x, grow up to 1.6x), choosing the cuts that keep every row nearest the target height, the last included (2026-10-15: greedy pooling left each month's last row wherever its days ran out; the owner: "make it so that images align on the right side"). A day that fits on one line keeps its photos together on it, and a day too wide for one line takes rows of its own; either gives way where that would leave a row short (2026-10-18, `internal/row-cuts`; the critique's "the Dell shows dead space": a day of two portraits between two days that each filled rows of their own had nothing to share a row with and stood alone, 389 of the Dell's 963px empty beside it). Then a day too wide for one line shares its first or last row with the days beside it, and one that fits one line breaks across two; breaking a day costs more than evening out any row is worth, so a day breaks only to fill one. Over the whole library (13,617 photos, the real layout over the photos' own ratios) rows short of the edge went from 85 to 0 on the Dell, 41 to 0 on the AORUS, 78 to 0 on a laptop and 121 to 0 on a phone, and rows reaching it only by widening their tiles, edges cropped, from 74 to 3 on the Dell (AORUS 37 to 0, laptop 97 to 4, phone 83 to 2). A day too wide for one line had kept a set number of lines, each justified whatever its height: up to 2.7x the target height on the Dell and 6.3x on a laptop (a lone portrait the full width); its rows keep to 0.72x to 1.6x now like any other. At other thumbnail sizes, short rows went from 66 to 0 (0.6) and 131 to 4 (1.9) on the Dell, and from 80 to 1 and 289 to 71 on a phone, where at 1.9 a lone portrait between wide photos has no row to share at 0.72x. **Months share rows** (2026-10-15, the owner's yes): a month whose last row of whole days cannot fill even at 1.6x hands it to the next month, which cuts those days in with its own first ones, where the next month has room for them beside its first photo (else the month fills that row itself); the handed-on days hang below their own month's box, exactly where the next month's box begins, so the row reads as one (`internal/layout-support`: a month box paints what overflows it and lets clicks through to its day sections; a month whose photos all go on that way has no height of its own). Sparse views fill the width now (Favorites: 33 of 48 rows short before, 2 of 58 after). Only where no next month can take them (the last month, or the next not yet loaded) does a short row widen its tiles, their edges cropped, by up to 1.35x, or stay short. A day that starts along a row and goes on to the next ones has its box from the row's start, over the photos of the days before it on that row, and the box lets the pointer through to them (`Month.svelte`, `data-wraps`). With day labels every day keeps rows of its own under its label, as before: a day too wide for one line has them justified to the edge, and a short day between two such stays short. Rows sit on whole pixels: the height is rounded and the widths take the hair that rounding left, so every full row ends on the same pixel (flooring the height had left most rows a few pixels short). While any day's layout is deferred, the upstream packing is the fallback.

**Where months start** (2026-10-16, the owner chose "Quiet month marks" over "Moments with places" and "Both, by zoom"). With months sharing rows and day labels off, nothing in the grid said where one month ends and the next begins. Each layout notes where a month starts (`internal/layout-support`, `TimelineMonth.monthStart`: its first photo in grid order, that photo's row and the gaps around it, worked out once per layout and after the months that place its handed-on days), and the month draws two quiet things from it (`components/timeline/Month.svelte`), neither of which moves a tile: **the month mark** on its first photo (Photo Tile) and **a hairline in the gap** where it starts, mid-gap on whole pixels, in ink at 30% (`--ws-month-line`, a step firmer than the 13% of a divider on the ground, which all but vanished in a 10px gap beside bright photos). A month that opens a row has the line across the whole width in the gap above that row; a month that starts mid-row has it as a corner, "┌": down the gap before its first photo for that row's height, and along the gap above the row from there to the grid's edge, over its own photos only. The line follows the day sections when they glide (same delay, none while the timeline scrolls or zooms). At the tightest gaps (Compact, the smallest sizes: 1-2px) the line all but fills the gap, and the mark carries the boundary. The timeline's first month has neither: the bar names it. **Day labels on:** no marks and no lines, since every day's label already dates it (the month changes in the label right above its first photo).

**Shell.** One bar (`components/omarchy/OmarchyBar.svelte`, 56px, one hairline along its foot, the glass when chosen): menu and mark in a first column as wide as the sidebar; the page's own content in the middle (`lib/managers/bar-slot` `content`; Photos puts its month heading, Years · Months and the years there, and with filters on the month heading alone, and its Years and Months overviews their name ("Years", "Months") with the span of years they lay out in data type ("1977–2026") and Years · Months · All (Time Strip), where the bar had stood empty on a phone and held the wordmark alone wider; every other page its title row: back button, title in the bar heading face, description in mono, then, where the page shows a timeline of days (Favorites, an album, a person, Archive, Trash, a Space's photos), the month in view ending the title row behind a hairline as tall as the text, Photos' own heading in muted ink on the title's baseline (`TimelineMonthHeading`, following that timeline through `managers/timeline-heading`; not the map's photo panel; the row's share of the bar grows by its room), then its own buttons, the row fitting itself to the bar (below)); then the icons: a page's own tool first (`bar-slot` `tools`; Photos and its overviews: the filter panel's reopen button), search as an icon that opens into the field over the middle, undo (only while there is something to take back: the recent actions, newest first, in a sheet like the View menu's; Ctrl+Z takes back the newest either way), the View menu (order, thumbnail size, day labels, expanded stacks, compact, then the theme: Follow Omarchy (the default) / Light / Dark, rounded corners, the inspector window (⇧I, see Inspector), then Cast), upload, notifications (on a phone in the account menu, below) and the account (28px, a ring at 38% ink with the initial in ink; a picture fills it; upstream's colour discs put the initial at 2.9:1 in dark themes). No second header row on the plain timeline. Sidebar shares the ground with a hairline edge. The filter rail starts collapsed; the timeline leads. **Theme and light/dark go together:** following Omarchy, the desktop picks light or dark (prefers-color-scheme, which Omarchy flips with its theme); a chosen theme brings its own; Light or Dark in the View menu holds one face until Follow Omarchy is picked again.

**One heading** (2026-10-18). The bar's title is the page's h1, with the look it had (the class draws it, not the element): a page's title (`UserPageLayout`), Photos' month heading (`TimelineMonthHeading`, an h1 where it heads the bar and plain text where it ends another page's title row), the Years and Months overviews' name. A page that heads its body with its name as a poster (the AI inbox, Command center, Chat, Ask) keeps the h1 in the poster, and the bar's name on a phone is text. Sections under it are h2s.

**Names that come through the bar's leading are h1s too** (2026-10-18; a probe found none on an album, a person or search results). ## Elevation & Depth

Flat on the ground; depth only for what floats above it, like a Hyprland popup. Upstream `shadow-sm/md/lg` utilities are zeroed. Tiles have no shadow; their edge is an inset hairline.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Popup** (`box-shadow: 0 12px 32px -8px rgb(0 0 0 / 0.28)` plus a 1px hairline border): menus, dialogs, listboxes, and the floating (mobile) grouping control.

### Named Rules
**The Floats Only Rule.** A shadow means the element floats over the ground and can be dismissed. Anything that lives on the ground is flat.

## Shapes

Corners come from the desktop: everything (tiles, menus, modals, toasts, fields, buttons, pills) uses the window radius `--ws-radius` exactly (Hyprland `decoration:rounding`; fallback 0px, square). Never add an offset to it: the owner rejected rounded controls on the square desktop (2026-10-12). A panel has one frame; separators run edge to edge. The last rules that added +4..10px were brought back to the token on 2026-10-13. Borders take the desktop's `--ws-border` (`general:border_size`, minimum 1px; fallback 2px) for focus, 1px for hairlines. Upstream's markup follows at the token: the whole Tailwind radius scale (`--radius-xs` to `--radius-4xl`) is `var(--ws-radius)` in `omarchy-world.css`, so stock cards, bars, fields and buttons square with the desktop; only `rounded-full` stays round, for faces and avatars. Photo tiles take the radius exactly at every size (the zoom-following ~6px of 2026-10-10 went on 2026-10-15). **Rounded corners** (the View menu's switch, per browser, 2026-10-16, `lib/theme/corners.svelte.ts`) is the one exception, and the owner's to choose: it puts `--ws-radius` at 8px whatever the theme (`:root[data-corners='rounded']`, set before the first paint by `app.html`), and the tiles on `--tile-radius`, which follows the zoom (~2px at the smallest size, 6px at the default, 8px for big tiles, halved in Compact) so the gaps between photos stay lines; faces stay round either way. Upstream's round controls (@immich/ui `shape="round"` icon buttons and buttons, control bars, switches, pill links) take it too; whatever holds a picture keeps its circle, so faces stay round, as do avatars, dots and spinners, which are not controls. The layer's own pills (the viewer's toolbar, stack badges, the drag count, Curator's tool pills and buttons, the compare handle) went square the same day; a brush ring, a spinner and a face stay round.

Tile focus is drawn **inset** (an inset box-shadow on a pseudo-element), because day sections use layout/paint containment and an outset border would clip at section edges.

## Motion

Things move the way the owner's desktop moves them. The theme hook reads Hyprland's animations (`hyprctl animations`) into motion tokens beside the geometry, so a window's animation on the desktop is a photo's animation here, and turning an animation off there turns it off here (0ms). `omarchy-world.css` carries the owner's values as fallbacks; `lib/utils/desktop-motion.ts` reads the same tokens for motion written in script.

- **Arriving: no motion, speed instead.** Photos, albums, folders and months are simply there when they come into view or a page opens. Two tries at an arrival animation were dropped on 2026-10-15: a window-style pop from the tile's first pixel played out unseen at the screen's edge, and one held until a fifth of the tile was in left blank strips and popped while the tile moved (the owner: "still animates a bit weird", then "or should we skip those animation and focus on speed"). Speed is the arrival now: the timeline makes tiles, and fetches their thumbnails, about a screen above and two below the view (`lib/utils/tunables.ts`, 1300px / 2600px; upstream 500px each), and Page Up / Page Down jump at once (`lib/utils/page-keys.ts`), so presses in a row, or the key held down, page as fast as they come onto photos already loaded.
- **Close** (`--ws-close-*` from windowsOut; popin 92%, 350ms): a photo that leaves (trash, archive, To do, Villa, QR) shrinks to the popin scale as it fades.
- **Re-tile** (`--ws-move-*` from windowsMove; 400ms on the snap curve `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`): the photos left behind glide into their new places, as windows do when one closes.
- **Workspace** (`--ws-workspace-*` from workspaces; slidefade 20%, 500ms snap): switching years (see Time Strip).
- **Favourite:** a heart given while the photo is on screen pops in from 40% on the open curve (0.65 of the open time); a heart already there when the tile is made just sits there.
- **Never in the way:** the timeline's own suspension (scrolling, zoom, relayout) stops close and re-tile, so nothing moves while the view does.
- **Reduced motion:** a leaving photo only fades (150ms at most) and the rest re-tile at once; years cut.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** read authenticated surfaces' colours from the runtime roles (`--immich-ui-light`, `--immich-ui-dark`, `--immich-ui-muted`, `--ws-accent`, `--ws-hairline`) and keep the fallbacks as fallbacks; reserve `public-memory-*` for the standalone recipient viewer.
- **Do** space tiles and derive header rhythm from `--ws-gap`; size tile corners and focus borders from `--ws-radius` and `--ws-border`.
- **Do** show the active/selected state by inversion (ink fill, surface text).
- **Do** set counts, years-as-data, dates and EXIF in the desktop monospace at 12px.
- **Do** give every motion a reduced-motion cut (no transition, no slide).
- **Do** style upstream markup through stable hooks and `ws-` classes so the layer rebases cleanly.

### Don't:
- **Don't** hard-code a theme's hex values, or add a brand colour of the layer's own.
- **Don't** use the accent as a fill for selected modes, nav items or years; it is the focus border.
- **Don't** put chrome on a second background tone or a rounded card slab; split the one ground with a hairline.
- **Don't** shadow anything that sits on the ground (tiles, rows, headers, the sidebar).
- **Don't** draw tile focus outset; it clips inside contained day sections.

### Person timelines: matching stack representatives (2026-10-19)

People pages fold existing stacks onto one matching photo: the real cover if it matches, otherwise the newest matching photo (ID breaks ties). This is a view only; it never changes the stack cover or deletes photos. The usual numbered badge expands only that person’s matching members. The existing Expand stacks preference still shows all matches.

`timeline-manager/person-stacks.ts` projects the same membership into day/month/year counts, lazy-loaded tiles and period covers. Viewer navigation from a folded child uses its representative; Add all to collection keeps the full matching count. Curation events reload the grouping and choose a new representative if necessary.

Curator’s authenticated `GET /api/people/{personId}/stacks` supplies the owner’s matching stack index. It intersects its read-only candidates with Gallery’s paginated person metadata search results, preserving Gallery’s visibility and access rules while including albums hidden from the home timeline. Shared photos owned by someone else remain ungrouped. Other accounts and an unavailable Curator retain the complete ungrouped timeline. Gallery’s server and native API are unchanged.

### Person photos and counts (2026-10-20)

The People list and a person's header count all matching photos, including the individual members of folded duplicate stacks. The header and Add all use the same total; day/month/year layout counts still describe the displayed tiles.

A person page loads its photos through Gallery's existing metadata search, paginating to completion and deduplicating IDs. Hiding an album from the home Photos timeline does not hide its matches on a face page. The search still enforces the viewer's access and excludes archived, locked, trashed, and offline assets. `timeline-manager/person-photos.ts` supplies the day/month/year buckets, covers, and normal timeline assets from that one result set, preserving capture dates and viewer navigation. Failed or cancelled searches cannot publish a partial count. This applies only to person pages; home timeline preferences and Gallery's server API stay unchanged.

### Editable face suggestion reviews (2026-10-19)

`PersonSuggestionReviewModal` keeps a draft choice per face while the review is open. Previous/Next, Left/Right arrow keys, or `[`/`]` revisit any buffered face, display its selected choice, and allow a replacement. Enter is the only global verdict shortcut and selects Same person; holding it does not advance through faces. Arrow keys never confirm, dismiss, or ignore a face. The end screen stays open so the last choice remains editable. Save and close, the close button, and Escape submit only each face's final choice through the existing native personal/Space APIs; unreviewed faces remain pending. Closing is the commit boundary, explicitly stated in the footer. Browser navigation/reload cannot silently discard pending choices.

Pagination advances normally while drafts leave the server queue intact. A partial save retains unsaved choices, marks successfully submitted faces Saved and read-only, and offers retry or discarding only the unsaved remainder. Retrying never replays successful requests. If reviewing continues after a partial save, pagination restarts at page 1 with ID deduplication because committed rows have shifted server offsets. This intentionally avoids compensating writes or engine changes to reverse an already committed face identity.

The review panel fills the available viewport height (with the usual desktop margin), and its footer never participates in photo sizing. The same verdict, navigation, and save controls remain mounted and in place during initial loading, portrait/landscape changes, revisiting a choice, saving, and completion. The photo uses a bounded `object-fit: contain` frame; its highlight follows frame resizes. Status space is reserved, save errors stay in the scrollable body, and the save label/helper reserve stable space. On very short screens only the body scrolls. This keeps repeated clicks on the same button safe, including at the final face where verdicts disable without being replaced by Save.


People list navigation stores the visited face's link and its position within the scroll panel, and restores the last loaded page inclusively before restoring that anchor (falling back to the scroll offset if the face is no longer listed). A resize observer keeps the face in place while the grid settles; the next pointer, wheel, touch, or keyboard action releases it and restores native scroll anchoring. The scroll action waits for Svelte to render those rows and abandons pending restoration if the page has been left. Returning through the person page's Back arrow or browser history therefore keeps the browsing position beyond the first 150 people, while preserving the active People/Pets filter and sort preference.
